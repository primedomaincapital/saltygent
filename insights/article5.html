The Quarter-Zip Is the Whole Fall Wardrobe: Layering the Coastal Way

THE QUARTER-ZIP · FALL 2026  ·  4 min read

The quarter-zip has quietly become the most important garment in coastal menswear, and Peter Millar's Autumn/Winter 2026 collection is the evidence. The brand, founded on the shores of North Carolina and celebrating its 25th year in 2026, built its reputation on the quarter-zip, and this season it has pushed the piece further from the golf course and deeper into everyday life. The showpiece is the Soft Suede performance quarter-zip, made from a blend of recycled polyester and modal with a sandwashed finish, designed to be layered over a polo and to blur the line between performance and lifestyle. Alongside it comes the Cradle Pique Performance Quarter-Zip, a double-knit piece with a textured piqué exterior and a smooth jersey interior, built for comfort and easy layering.

The rest of the collection fills in around it. The Featherweight line, known for its ultra-lightweight feel and UPF50+ sun protection, expands beyond polos into hoodies, trousers, t-shirts, and shorts. The Cradle jumper returns, textured outside and buttery soft inside, and the Ardent Air mid-layer provides insulation for days that call for more than a quarter-zip alone. The Perth and Pine jumpers return as versatile layering options that breathe and regulate temperature without weighing the wearer down. The ETC polo, made with a performance fabric that wicks sweat while keeping the hand feel and matte look of a jersey knit cotton polo, anchors the base layer.

How the layers actually work

The formula is simple enough to remember on a cold morning. Start with a moisture-wicking polo under a four-way-stretch quarter-zip. Add trousers that allow a full rotation, then use an insulated vest when the torso needs warmth but the arms need freedom. The combination handles a temperature swing well because the vest and the quarter-zip can come off without disrupting the outfit. The Perth Performance Quarter-Zip and the Essex Vest create the familiar fall pairing, while Excursionist Flex and merino-blend options provide a softer finish for dry conditions.

Color does the quiet work. Navy, charcoal, olive, and similar restrained shades connect the polo, the mid-layer, the trousers, and the vest without forcing a new wardrobe theme. The Fall 2026 palette at Darien Sport Shop, the Connecticut institution that has sold Peter Millar since the brand's early days, runs to forest green, brown, and plum in the quarter-zips, with new pant colors in Reno Wolf and Arena Wolf, long-sleeve merino polos with mother-of-pearl buttons, and a reversible vest with a merino wool exterior and a water-resistant interior. Three colors in the quarter-zip, three more in the trousers, and the whole closet starts talking to itself.

The layering logic extends beyond the course. A light blue quarter-zip over a checkered shirt with a gray vest and blue trousers carries a man from the office to dinner; darker earth tones with a navy crewneck and a quilted olive vest cover the weekend. The pieces are designed to be taken off as often as they are put on, which is the whole point of a coastal wardrobe: the weather changes, and the outfit changes with it, without a trip home.

The counterargument: the quarter-zip problem

There is a case against the quarter-zip, and it is worth hearing, because it is the reason the piece needs judgment. The quarter-zip is the unofficial uniform of American finance, tech, and conference culture, and a man who wears one without thinking can look like he is dressed for a company offsite rather than a coastal weekend. The vest-over-quarter-zip combination, in particular, has become so common that it reads as a costume when the colors are loud, the logo is large, and the fit is boxy.

The answer is not to abandon the piece. It is to wear it the way the coastal brands intended: fitted rather than boxy, in muted colors, with the collar sitting flat and the zip closed or open by a few inches, never halfway. A quarter-zip in navy over a white or light blue polo, with well-cut chinos and clean loafers, reads as a man who dressed with intent. The same piece in a bright color with a chest logo reads as a giveaway. The difference is not the garment. It is the discipline.

What to buy, and what to skip

Buy one quarter-zip in navy and one in charcoal or olive, in a performance knit that breathes and holds its shape. Buy a quilted vest in a color that works with both. Buy the merino polo with the mother-of-pearl buttons, because the details are what separate a $200 quarter-zip from a $40 one. Skip the loud colors, the big logos, and the third quarter-zip in a shade you already own. The coastal wardrobe is a small wardrobe, worn well, and the quarter-zip earns its place in it by doing the work of three garments — base, middle, and outer — depending on the hour and the wind.

Fall on the coast is a season of changing light and changing weather, and the men who dress best for it are the ones who plan for the change instead of fighting it. The quarter-zip is the plan, worn on the shoulders.
